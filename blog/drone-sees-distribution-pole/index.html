What a Drone Sees on a Distribution Pole

By Utilisight Corp·Nov 07, 2026·1 min read

A drone carrying both a visual (RGB) camera and a radiometric thermal camera captures two views of the same pole. Visual images show physical condition; thermal images show components that may be running hotter than they should. Every finding is flagged for confirmation by qualified line crews.

1. Connectors and splices

  • Visual: loose or displaced hardware, corrosion, broken or bird-caged strands.
  • Thermal: warmer spots that may indicate high resistance from looseness or corrosion.

2. Fuse cutouts and switches

  • Visual: cracked barrels, misaligned blades, tracking marks.
  • Thermal: heating at hinge or clip contacts, which may point to loose or corroded contacts.

3. Pole-mounted transformers

  • Visual: oil stains or leaks, chipped bushings, rust on the tank.
  • Thermal: heating at bushing connections. Unusual heat patterns on the tank are flagged for follow-up, and the oil level can sometimes be seen as a temperature boundary.

4. Surge arresters

  • Visual: cracks, separated ground-lead disconnectors, contamination.
  • Thermal: an arrester warmer than its neighbours under normal voltage may indicate internal deterioration and should be checked promptly.

5. Insulators

  • Visual: chips, cracks, contamination, tracking marks, damaged tie wires.
  • Thermal: heating along the surface that may indicate leakage current.

6. Crossarms and hardware

  • Visual: splits, rot, bent braces, loose bolts.
  • Thermal: leakage current from a failing insulator into a wood crossarm heats the wood, and in some cases can start a pole-top fire.
