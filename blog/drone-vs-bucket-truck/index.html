Drone, Bucket Truck or Foot Patrol: Which Fits?

By Utilisight Corp·Nov 07, 2026·2 min read

Each method has its strengths. Drones don't replace line crews; they help crews spend less time searching and more time fixing.

Foot patrol Bucket truck Drone
Coverage speed Slow Moderate Fast for screening many poles
Access Walkable areas Drivable right-of-way Limited by line of sight, airspace and people below
View of the pole top From below, often blocked Close-up From above and the side
Hands-on repair No Yes No
Crew exposure Low Higher: height and energized equipment Low: operated from the ground

Roles

  • Drones: screening. Capture thermal and visual images of pole tops without climbing or trucks, flag anomalies and locate them pole by pole.
  • Bucket trucks: confirmation and repair. Sent to flagged locations instead of inspecting every pole.
  • Foot patrols: ground level. Pole bases, guy wires, vegetation and right-of-way conditions are best seen from the ground.

A combined workflow

  1. Drone flight: thermal and visual images are captured and GPS-tagged.
  2. Thermographer review: anomalies are ranked by severity and located on a map.
  3. Crew follow-up: line crews confirm and repair flagged components.

Drone flights stay within visual line of sight and away from people below, so urban routes are planned around roads and sidewalks.
