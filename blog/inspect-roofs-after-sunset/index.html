Why We Inspect Roofs After Sunset

By Utilisight Corp·Nov 07, 2026·2 min read

If you book a thermal roof inspection, you may be surprised that it's scheduled at night or very early in the morning. There's good science behind the timing.

Different materials store heat differently

During the day, the sun heats everything on the roof: the membrane, fasteners, insulation and any trapped water. Materials store and release heat at different rates, a property called thermal capacitance.

  • Dry insulation has low thermal capacitance. It heats up quickly in the sun and cools quickly after sunset.
  • Water and wet insulation have high thermal capacitance. They heat slowly and hold that heat for hours.

Why not in the afternoon?

On a sunny afternoon, a dark roof membrane can exceed 65 °C (150 °F). Direct sun heats the whole surface and hides the small temperature differences that point to wet insulation.

After sunset, the surface cools. Dry areas cool quickly, while areas with trapped moisture stay warmer. That contrast is what the camera picks up.

Choosing the window

The best window usually opens once the surface has cooled after sunset and can extend into the early morning. Conditions on the night decide:

  • Clear sky: heat radiates off the roof faster, increasing contrast.
  • Low wind: wind cools the surface evenly and hides patterns.
  • Dry surface: no rain, dew, frost or standing water.
  • No sun on the roof: even low sun can create false readings.

What you receive

To the eye, the roof looks uniform. In thermal, cooler areas typically appear in dark tones and warmer areas in bright tones. The inspection is done by drone, with no roof access needed. Each warm area is located on a roof plan with paired thermal and visual images, so your roofer or consultant knows where to investigate.
