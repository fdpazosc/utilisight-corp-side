What a Thermal Roof Scan Can (and Can't) Tell You

By Utilisight Corp·Nov 07, 2026·3 min read

When a commercial roof starts to age or leak, knowing what's happening under the membrane helps you spend repair money in the right place. Infrared thermography, often called a thermal roof scan, is one of the most effective non-destructive ways to screen a roof for trapped moisture.

It's also often misunderstood. Some treat it like an X-ray that finds every leak; others dismiss it as a pretty picture. Knowing what it can and can't do helps you get real value from it.

What a thermal roof scan can tell you

Thermal cameras don't see through roofs, and they don't see water directly. They measure surface temperature. During the day, trapped water absorbs and stores heat. After sunset, dry insulation cools quickly while wet insulation holds heat longer. That difference shows up on the roof surface.

  • The approximate extent of suspected wet areas. Instead of guessing how far water has spread from a leak, a scan outlines areas that may hold wet insulation. This helps plan targeted repairs instead of a full tear-off.
  • Possible moisture before it reaches the interior. Water can travel several metres along the deck before it drips inside. A scan can flag suspected wet areas before ceilings, equipment or inventory are damaged.
  • Priorities for capital planning. Across one large roof or several buildings, a scan helps rank which areas to investigate first.
  • A check after repairs. A follow-up scan can show whether thermal anomalies remain after wet material is replaced.

What a thermal roof scan can't tell you

  • Where the water entered. A scan shows where moisture may be trapped, not where it came in. Water can enter through a small puncture 3 m away and settle in a low spot. Finding the entry point takes a physical inspection.
  • The cause of every warm spot. Not every anomaly is wet insulation. Ducts, piping, changes in insulation thickness, concrete supports, ballast and debris can all create similar patterns.
  • How wet the insulation is. Thermal data shows warmer and cooler areas, not moisture content. Measuring how wet insulation is requires physical testing such as core samples or moisture meters.

A map, not a diagnosis

Think of a thermal scan as a map that shows where to look. A qualified roofer or building envelope consultant confirms the findings with physical testing before deciding on repairs. Used that way, a scan helps avoid unnecessary replacements and focuses repair budgets where they matter.
