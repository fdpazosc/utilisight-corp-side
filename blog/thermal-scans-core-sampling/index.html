Thermal Scans and Core Sampling: How They Work Together

By Utilisight Corp·Nov 07, 2026·X min read

Thermal imaging is one of the most useful screening tools for low-slope commercial roofs. Building envelope consultants also know that thermal data alone can be misread. The most reliable approach has two steps: a thermal scan to find where to look, and core samples to confirm what's there.

Step 1: The thermal scan

  • Full coverage: a scan covers the whole accessible roof area, not just a sampling grid.
  • Non-destructive: suspected wet areas are outlined without cutting the membrane.
  • Targeted sampling: cores go where anomalies are, not at random.

Thermal anomalies don't always mean moisture. Common look-alikes include:

  • Ducts or piping below the deck
  • Changes in insulation thickness
  • Concrete structure next to lighter steel deck
  • Ballast, debris or surface material changes that affect emissivity

Step 2: Core sampling

A core cut through the roof assembly confirms what the scan suggests:

  • Moisture presence: whether a thermal anomaly is wet insulation or something else.
  • Roof assembly: cover board, insulation type, vapour retarder and layers.
  • Deck condition: corrosion on steel, spalling on concrete or rot on wood beneath wet areas.
  • Moisture content: lab testing by weight, when needed for warranty or legal disputes.

A combined workflow

Coring without a scan is guesswork: more cuts, missed wet areas and an incomplete picture. A report based only on unverified thermal images risks recommendations that lead to unnecessary tear-offs or failed repairs.

A common approach:

  1. Scan the roof under suitable conditions: after sunset, low wind, clear sky and a dry surface.
  2. Locate each thermal anomaly on a roof plan.
  3. Take cores in representative anomaly areas, plus at least one in a dry-reading reference area.
  4. Repair every core site with compatible materials.
  5. Compare the thermal map with the core results in the final assessment.

Utilisight provides steps 1 and 2 as an independent third party. Cores and repairs are carried out by your consultant or roofer.
