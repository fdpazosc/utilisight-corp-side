Is Winter a Good Time for a Roof Thermal Scan?

By Utilisight Corp·Nov 07, 2026·2 min read

Many property owners assume roof work ends in November. For thermal roof scans, cold weather can be an advantage.

Why cold weather helps

Radiometric cameras estimate surface temperature, but what matters most is the difference (ΔT) between neighbouring areas. The bigger the difference between dry and wet areas, the easier suspected moisture is to see.

  • Building heat. In winter, heated buildings push warmth up through the roof. Dry insulation slows that heat. Wet insulation loses much of its insulating value, so more heat reaches the surface and wet areas can stand out against a cold roof.
  • Clear nights. Winter often brings clear, dry nights, when the roof surface loses heat quickly to the sky. That increases contrast.

What winter scans need

  • No snow, ice or standing water on the membrane. Snow insulates the surface and hides patterns.
  • A heated building. Interior heat drives the pattern, so the building should be at normal operating temperature.
  • Low wind and a dry surface, as in any season.
  • Suitable flying weather. Extreme cold shortens drone battery life, so flights are planned around suitable nights.

Don't wait for spring

Water trapped in a roof can freeze and thaw repeatedly through winter, which can damage insulation and membranes. A late-fall or early-winter scan helps identify suspected wet areas so you can plan repairs and spring budgets early.
